# The Ship That Couldn't Sit Still — script v2

## Facts (with sources)
1. Sweden's King Gustav II Adolf ordered the Vasa. It was built in Stockholm from 1626 to 1628 and carried 64 bronze cannons on two gun decks, which was unusual for a Swedish ship of the time. — Vasa Museum, "The ship" (vasamuseet.se); Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Vasa".
2. It carried hundreds of painted carved sculptures, with a large lion figurehead at the bow. — Vasa Museum, "Sculptures".
3. **Stability test:** before the voyage, Admiral Klas Fleming had 30 men run back and forth across the deck. He stopped the test after 3 runs because the ship was heeling so far that it would have capsized. She sailed anyway. — Vasa Museum, "Why did Vasa sink?"; inquiry records summarised in Britannica.
4. **Maiden voyage:** Sunday 10 August 1628. Crowds watched from the shore. After about 1,300 m, a gust of wind heeled her over, water poured in through the open lower gunports, and she sank in Stockholm harbour. About 30 people died. — Vasa Museum; Britannica.
5. **Inquiry:** no one was punished. The master shipwright, Henrik Hybertsson, had died in 1627, before she was finished, and the king had approved the dimensions. — Vasa Museum, "The inquiry".
6. **Cause:** too little ballast (stones in the hold) for the weight of the cannons up high. The centre of gravity was too high and the hull too narrow at the waterline. — Vasa Museum.
7. **Twist:** found by Anders Franzén in 1956 and raised on 24 April 1961, about 98 % original. It is now the Vasa Museum, the most visited museum in Scandinavia (over 1 million visitors a year). — Vasa Museum, "Visitor statistics"; Britannica.
8. The king was away fighting the Polish-Swedish War (1626-29) in Polish Prussia and was pressing for the ship to be ready. — Britannica, "Gustav II Adolf"; Vasa Museum.

> Check these against the sources before recording. One that needs care: the death toll is estimated at 30-50, so the script avoids giving a number.
