## Facts (with sources)
1. Vasa was built in Stockholm for King Gustav II Adolf. At her maiden voyage she carried 64 cannons on two gun decks; the museum calls her "the most powerfully armed ship in the Baltic, if not the world". — [Vasa Museum: Machine of war](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/machine-of-war); [Facts about Vasa](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/facts-about-vasa)
2. She carried hundreds of carved sculptures (the museum counts 460 sculptures and about 300 ornaments), brightly painted with abundant gold leaf; the figurehead is a three-metre lion holding the Vasa coat of arms. — [Vasa Museum: Art](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/art); [Vasa Up Close](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/visit/exhibitions/new-exhibition-vasa-up-close)
3. **Stability test**, probably 8 or 9 July 1628 (about five weeks before sailing): thirty men ran back and forth across the upper deck while Vice Admiral Klas Fleming watched; after just a few runs she heeled so much that he had the test stopped, afraid she would capsize at the quay. Under pressure from the king, she was sent to sea anyway. — [Vasa Museum: The Inquest](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/inquest); [Timeline](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/timeline)
4. **Maiden voyage**: Sunday 10 August 1628, calm weather with occasional gusts, a large audience watching. After about 1,300 metres a stronger gust at Tegelviken heeled her to port and water poured in through the open lower gunports; she sank in Stockholm harbour within minutes. About 30 people died (contemporary letters say 30-50). — [Vasa Museum: The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster); [Timeline](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/timeline); [FAQ: How many people died?](https://faq.vasamuseet.se/category/45/article/1282?lang=en)
5. **The king** was with his army on the other side of the Baltic (in Prussia, during the war with Poland). — [Vasa Museum: The Inquest](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/inquest); [FAQ: The ship & its history](https://faq.vasamuseet.se/category/45?lang=en)
6. **Inquiry**: an inquest looked for someone to blame. The designer, master shipwright Henrik Hybertsson, had died in 1627, more than a year before the voyage; the builders had followed dimensions the king approved, and no one was convicted. — [Vasa Museum: The Inquest](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/inquest); [Audio guide 8: The Inquiry](https://www.vasamuseet.se/besok/audioguide/audioguide-english/textversion/8.-the-inquiry)
7. **Cause**: the inquest found neither the ballast (she carried 120 tonnes of stone) nor loose cannons to blame; she was badly designed, with upperworks too tall and heavily built for the relatively small hull below the waterline. — [Vasa Museum: The Inquest](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/inquest); [The Disaster](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/explore/vasa-history/disaster)
8. **Salvage**: found by Anders Franzén in 1956, she broke the surface on the morning of 24 April 1961 (1628 + 333 = 1961). — [Vasa Museum: The Salvaging](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/visit/exhibitions/salvaging); [FAQ: When was Vasa salvaged?](https://faq.vasamuseet.se/category/45/article/1283?lang=en)
9. **Today**: about 1.5 million visitors a year; the museum and Visit Sweden describe it as the most visited museum in Scandinavia (a self-reported claim). — [About the Vasa Museum](https://www.vasamuseet.se/en/about-the-vasa-museum); [Visiting the Vasa Museum 2025 (PDF)](https://www.vasamuseet.se/globalassets/vasamuseet/dokument/bransch/2025/vasa-museum_2025_english_2.pdf); [Visit Sweden: Stockholm](https://visitsweden.com/where-to-go/middle-sweden/stockholm/week-long-city-break/)
